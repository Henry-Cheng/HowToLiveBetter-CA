# 22. 怎么放松：娱乐场所和减压 / How to Relax: Entertainment Venues and Stress Relief

前半节讲娱乐场所里哪些钱花得冤、安全出口在哪，口径是金钱和人身自由。后半节讲怎么减压，口径是精力和总死亡率。两半的数字不放在一起换算。第 3 节已经写过情绪低落时先做哪几件事，这里给更具体的做法。

The first half covers entertainment venues — where money gets wasted and where the emergency exits are — measured in dollars and personal freedom. The second half covers how to de-stress, measured in energy and all-cause mortality. The numbers in the two halves aren't converted into each other. Section 3 already covered what to do first when you're feeling low; here are more concrete approaches.

### 1. 进 karaoke、酒吧、escape room 之前先看一眼安全出口在哪，被锁被堵就换一家 / Check the emergency exits before entering a karaoke bar, pub, or escape room — if they're locked or blocked, go somewhere else
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- 成本：不花钱。进门时抬头看 10 秒。
  Cost: Free. Look up for 10 seconds when you walk in.
- 说人话：加州的消防法规同样要求营业场所的疏散通道和安全出口畅通，紧急出口不能上锁、不能被杂物堵住，指示标志不能遮挡。包厢在地下、只有一个出口、走道堆满杂物的，换一家。
  In plain terms: California fire codes likewise require businesses to keep evacuation routes and emergency exits clear — exit doors must not be locked or blocked by clutter, and exit signs must not be covered. If the room is in a basement, has only one exit, or the hallway is piled with stuff, go somewhere else.
- 收益：消防法规要求营业场所保证疏散通道和安全出口畅通，不得封堵、锁闭，不得在通道上设置影响疏散的障碍物，疏散指示标志不得遮挡（具体条款待核实）。着火或被困时打 911。
  Benefit: Fire codes require businesses to keep evacuation routes and emergency exits clear — no blocking, no locking, no obstacles that hinder evacuation — and exit signs must not be covered (specific code sections to be verified). Call 911 in a fire or if trapped.
- 证据等级：A / Evidence grade: A
- 来源：加州消防法规（营业场所紧急出口畅通要求；具体条款待核实）
- 备注：下面几样占了任何一样，就换个地方：包厢在地下、只有一个出口、走道堆满杂物、安全出口的指示灯不亮。这类场所着火时烟大、走道窄、人多，而且多数人喝了酒。留给你逃出去的时间以秒计。
  Note: If any of these is true, go somewhere else: the room is in a basement, there's only one exit, the hallway is cluttered, or the exit sign isn't lit. Fires in these places produce heavy smoke, the hallways are narrow and crowded, and most people have been drinking. The time you have to get out is measured in seconds.

### 2. 点单前要价目表，娱乐场所必须明码标价，不接受「先玩后算」 / Ask for the price list before ordering — don't accept "play now, pay later"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- 成本：不花钱。开口要一次单子。难在当着人开口问价，有点抹不开面子。
  Cost: Free. Just ask for the price list once. The hard part is asking in front of others — it feels awkward.
- 说人话：在美国，所谓「天价账单」几乎都出在没看过价目表、事后按口头报价结账的场合。进包厢先要菜单或价目表，点完再要一份确认清单。有争议先别付款，要求按价目表结算。
  In plain terms: In the U.S., outrageous bills almost always happen when nobody saw a price list and the tab is settled on verbal quotes afterward. Ask for the menu or price list when you sit down, and ask for a confirmed itemized list after ordering. If there's a dispute, don't pay yet — insist on settling per the price list.
- 收益：加州的消费者保护法规禁止价格欺诈，也要求广告价格包含全部强制收费（具体条款待核实）。绝大多数「天价酒水」纠纷都发生在这种场合：没有出示价目表，事后按口头报价结账。消费者投诉可以找加州消费者事务局（Department of Consumer Affairs）。
  Benefit: California consumer-protection law prohibits price fraud and requires advertised prices to include all mandatory fees (specific provisions to be verified). The vast majority of "outrageous bar tab" disputes happen in this situation: no price list shown, settled on verbal quotes afterward. Consumer complaints can go to the California Department of Consumer Affairs.
- 证据等级：A / Evidence grade: A
- 来源：加州消费者保护法规（价格欺诈与明码标价相关条款；具体条款待核实）
- 备注：怎么做：进包厢先要一份价目表，纸质的电子的都行，拿到就拍照。点完单再要一份确认好的清单。服务员推荐的「特调」「果盘」，一律先问价再点。结账有争议时先别付款。别先付款再去维权，付了款你要举证就难得多。
  Note: How to do it: ask for a price list when you sit down — paper or digital, photograph it once you have it. Ask for a confirmed list after ordering. For anything the server recommends ("specials," fruit plates), ask the price first. Don't pay before a billing dispute is resolved. Don't pay first and dispute later — once you've paid, proving your case is much harder.

### 3. 场子里有人递「东西」就立刻走，容留和提供都不是「帮朋友」 / If someone offers you "stuff" at a venue, leave immediately — letting it happen isn't "helping a friend"
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- 成本：不花钱。站起来走出去。难在要当着一桌人的面离席。
  Cost: Free. Stand up and walk out. The hard part is leaving in front of the whole table.
- 说人话：在美国，娱乐场所里贩毒、提供毒品都触犯毒品法律，场地会被吊销执照、关闭。你自己用也要被抓、留案底。在你订的包厢或住处让人「用一下」，你自己也可能被追究（具体法律后果待核实）。有人掏出不明粉末、片剂、烟弹请你试，当场走。
  In plain terms: In the U.S., selling or providing drugs at entertainment venues violates drug laws — the venue can lose its license and be shut down. Using yourself gets you arrested and a criminal record. Letting someone "use" drugs in a room you booked or at your place can get you in legal trouble too (exact consequences to be verified). If someone pulls out an unknown powder, pill, or vape cartridge and asks you to try it, walk out on the spot.
- 收益：联邦和加州法律都禁止制造、贩卖、持有受管制物质（毒品），娱乐场所涉毒会被吊销执照、关闭整顿。个人持有、使用受管制物质会被逮捕起诉，留刑事记录。在自己订的包厢或住处容留他人吸毒，可能被追究刑事责任（待核实）。
  Benefit: Federal and California law both prohibit manufacturing, selling, or possessing controlled substances (drugs); venues involved with drugs can lose their licenses and be shut down. Personal possession or use of controlled substances leads to arrest, prosecution, and a criminal record. Letting others use drugs in a room you booked or at your residence may bring criminal liability (to be verified).
- 证据等级：A / Evidence grade: A
- 来源：联邦与加州管制物质法规（具体条款待核实）
- 备注：只要有人拿出不明的粉末、片剂、电子烟弹，请你「试试」，就当场离开，别留下来看热闹。你个人担的风险比场所更重。
  Note: The moment someone brings out an unknown powder, pill, or vape cartridge and asks you to "try it," leave on the spot — don't stay to watch. The risk you personally carry is heavier than the venue's.

### 4. 不吃陌生人给的糖和零食，不喝离开过视线的饮料，不接别人递的烟弹 / Don't eat candy or snacks from strangers, don't drink a beverage that left your sight, don't accept vapes others hand you
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- 成本：不花钱。别人递过来的一律不接。自己的杯子离开过视线，就换一杯。
  Cost: Free. Decline everything others hand you. If your cup has been out of your sight, get a fresh one.
- 说人话：在美国也有人在酒吧、karaoke 派对上递掺了东西的电子烟、软糖或饮料，样子和普通的东西差不多。饮料离开过视线就换一杯，陌生人递的吃的、喝的、烟弹一律不接。
  In plain terms: In the U.S., people also hand out spiked vapes, gummies, or drinks at bars and karaoke parties — they look just like the ordinary stuff. If your drink has been out of your sight, get a new one; decline all food, drinks, and vape cartridges from strangers.
- 收益：掺了毒品或迷奸药的饮料、糖果、电子烟油在美国也出现过。被下药之后常跟着抢劫和性侵。饮料只要离开过视线就换一杯，比事后追究省事得多。
  Benefit: Drinks, candy, and vape oils laced with drugs or date-rape drugs have shown up in the U.S. too. Being drugged is often followed by robbery or sexual assault. Getting a fresh drink whenever yours leaves your sight is far less trouble than dealing with the aftermath.
- 证据等级：A / Evidence grade: A
- 来源：（美国相应警示与法规来源待核实）
- 备注：不只是娱乐场所，学校派对、朋友聚会同理。给的是糖果、饮料还是烟弹，也都一样。场子里有人拿出不明粉末、片剂、烟弹时怎么办，见本节第 3 条。替人带东西见第 8 节第 29 条。
  Note: This applies beyond entertainment venues — school parties and friend gatherings too. Whether it's candy, a drink, or a vape cartridge, it's the same. For what to do when someone brings out an unknown powder, pill, or cartridge at a venue, see item 3 of this section. For carrying things for others, see section 8, item 29.

### 5. escape room 别去开在居民楼里或者地下室深层的 / Don't go to escape rooms in residential buildings or deep basement levels
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- 成本：不花钱。订之前问一句地址在几层。
  Cost: Free. Ask which floor it's on before booking.
- 说人话：escape room 这类场地黑、隔断多、道具易燃，门还常常要解谜才打得开。一旦起火，比普通商铺难跑得多。订之前看一眼地址和楼层，开在居民楼里、地下室深层的就换一家。
  In plain terms: Escape rooms are dark, full of partitions, with flammable props — and the doors often only open after you solve a puzzle. In a fire, they're much harder to escape than an ordinary shop. Check the address and floor before booking; skip venues in residential buildings or deep basement levels.
- 收益：这类场所的特点是黑暗、隔断多、道具易燃，门常常需要「解谜」才能打开。一旦起火，比普通商铺更难跑出去。消防法规要求经营场所保持疏散通道畅通（具体条款待核实），不合规的换一家。
  Benefit: These venues are characteristically dark, partitioned, and full of flammable props, with doors that often require "solving a puzzle" to open. In a fire they're far harder to escape than an ordinary shop. Fire codes require businesses to keep evacuation routes clear (specific provisions to be verified); skip venues that don't comply.
- 证据等级：A / Evidence grade: A
- 来源：加州消防法规（经营场所疏散通道要求；具体条款待核实）
- 备注：订之前在点评页面上就能看出楼层，也能看出是不是居民楼。不合规的直接换一家。
  Note: Review pages usually show the floor and whether it's in a residential building before you book. Skip non-compliant venues outright.

### 6. 心情差就去走或者跑，抗抑郁的效应量（效果大小）跟强度成正比 / When you're feeling down, walk or run — the antidepressant effect grows with intensity
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- 成本：不花钱。每次半小时上下。难在情绪差的时候最不想动，得推自己一把。
  Cost: Free. About half an hour each time. The hard part is that when you feel worst, you least want to move — you have to push yourself.
- 说人话：心情差的时候，快走或慢跑改善最明显。其次是瑜伽、力量训练、太极气功，几种差距不大。强度开得越高效果越好，力量训练和瑜伽最容易坚持。这方面的证据质量偏低，能确定的是动比不动好，挑自己坚持得下来的那种。
  In plain terms: When you're feeling down, brisk walking or jogging helps the most. Next come yoga, strength training, and tai chi/qigong, with little difference between them. Higher intensity works better, and strength training and yoga are the easiest to stick with. The evidence quality here is on the low side; what's certain is that moving beats not moving — pick the kind you can keep doing.
- 收益：这是把 218 项随机分成两组的对照试验合在一起算的结果。涉及 495 个组、14170 名符合重度抑郁临床标准的参与者。算法让各种运动互相比较（贝叶斯网状荟萃分析）。拿来对比的是常规照护和安慰剂片（主动对照）。效果大小用 Hedges' g 表示，数越往负走，症状改善越多。快走或慢跑 −0.62（95% 可信区间 −0.80 至 −0.45，意思是真实值大致落在这一段里）。瑜伽 −0.55（−0.73 至 −0.36）。力量训练 −0.49（−0.69 至 −0.29）。混合有氧 −0.43（−0.61 至 −0.24）。太极或气功 −0.42（−0.65 至 −0.21）。原文说「运动的效果与所处方的强度成正比」：强度开得越高，效果越好。力量训练和瑜伽的可接受度最好，最容易坚持下来。
  Benefit: This pools 218 randomized controlled trials: 495 arms, 14,170 participants meeting clinical criteria for major depression, compared against each other with a Bayesian network meta-analysis, using usual care and placebo pills (active controls) as comparators. Effect sizes are in Hedges' g — the more negative, the more symptoms improved. Brisk walking or jogging: −0.62 (95% credible interval −0.80 to −0.45, i.e., the true value roughly falls in this range). Yoga: −0.55 (−0.73 to −0.36). Strength training: −0.49 (−0.69 to −0.29). Mixed aerobic exercise: −0.43 (−0.61 to −0.24). Tai chi or qigong: −0.42 (−0.65 to −0.21). The paper states "the effect of exercise was proportional to the intensity prescribed": higher intensity, better results. Strength training and yoga had the best acceptability — easiest to stick with.
- 证据等级：A / Evidence grade: A
- 来源：Noetel M, Sanders T, Gallardo-Gómez D, et al. (2024). Effect of exercise for depression: systematic review and network meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials. BMJ, 384, e075847. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847>
- 备注：争议。作者自己写明，218 项研究里只有 1 项做得够干净、不容易被设计带偏。按通行的打分法，快走慢跑只算「低」可信，其余是「极低」。运动也瞒不住参与者，人知道自己在锻炼，就容易觉得好些，这份期待去不掉。结论方向仍然可用：动比不动好，强度别太低，选自己能坚持的那种。这是第 3 节「情绪低落时先动起来」的展开，不能替代确诊后的规范治疗。
  Note: Contested. The authors themselves note that only 1 of the 218 studies was clean enough to be relatively free of design bias. By standard grading, brisk walking/jogging counts as "low" certainty and the rest "very low." Exercise can't be blinded — people know they're exercising, which makes them feel better, and that expectation can't be removed. The direction of the conclusion still holds: moving beats not moving, don't go too easy, pick what you can stick with. This expands on section 3's "move first when you're feeling low" and doesn't replace proper treatment after a diagnosis.

### 7. 焦虑到影响生活时，正念减压课的效果不劣于常用的抗焦虑药 / When anxiety disrupts your life, a mindfulness stress-reduction course works no worse than common anti-anxiety medication
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- 成本：报课要花一点钱。课程 8 周，每周上一次课，还要每天在家练。难在连着 8 周每天都练。
  Cost: The course costs some money. It's 8 weeks, one class per week, plus daily practice at home. The hard part is practicing every day for 8 straight weeks.
- 说人话：确诊焦虑障碍的人上 8 周正念减压课，症状改善和吃抗焦虑药艾司西酞普兰（escitalopram）几乎一样。吃药的人近八成出过不良反应，有人因此退出。上课的人约一成半出过，没人退出。课得是标准化的 8 周课程，随便一个冥想 App 不算。
  In plain terms: For people diagnosed with an anxiety disorder, an 8-week mindfulness-based stress reduction course improved symptoms about as much as the anti-anxiety drug escitalopram. Nearly 80% of the medication group had side effects, and some dropped out because of them. About 15% of the course group had side effects, and nobody dropped out. It has to be a standardized 8-week course — a random meditation app doesn't count.
- 收益：这是美国 3 家城市学术医疗中心做的试验，目的是看上课会不会比吃药差（非劣效随机对照试验）。276 名确诊焦虑障碍的成年人按 1:1 随机对半分组。一组上 8 周正念减压课程。一组吃艾司西酞普兰（10 至 20 mg 弹性剂量）。208 人做完全程。结束时按医生打的焦虑严重程度分（临床总体印象严重度量表）算，正念组下降 1.35，药物组下降 1.43。两组差 −0.07（95% CI −0.38 至 0.23，即真实差距大致落在这一段里；P=0.65，即这点差距和没差别看不出区别）。这一段的下限没有越过事先划好的「算不上更差」的线（非劣效界值 −0.495）。副作用差别很大：药物组 78.6% 至少出现一次和试验有关的不良反应，10 人因此退出。正念组 15.4%，无人因此退出。
  Benefit: This trial ran at 3 urban academic medical centers in the U.S., designed to see whether the course would be worse than medication (a non-inferiority randomized controlled trial). 276 adults diagnosed with anxiety disorders were randomized 1:1. One group took an 8-week mindfulness-based stress reduction course; the other took escitalopram (10–20 mg flexible dosing). 208 completed the full course. At the end, clinician-rated anxiety severity (Clinical Global Impression–Severity scale) dropped 1.35 in the mindfulness group and 1.43 in the medication group — a difference of −0.07 (95% CI −0.38 to 0.23, i.e., the true gap roughly falls in this range; P=0.65, meaning this gap is indistinguishable from no difference). The lower end of this range did not cross the pre-set "counts as not worse" line (non-inferiority margin −0.495). Side effects differed sharply: 78.6% of the medication group had at least one trial-related adverse event, and 10 dropped out because of them. In the mindfulness group it was 15.4%, with no dropouts.
- 证据等级：A / Evidence grade: A
- 来源：Hoge EA, Bui E, Mete M, Dutton MA, Baker AW, Simon NM (2023). Mindfulness-based stress reduction vs escitalopram for the treatment of adults with anxiety disorders: a randomized clinical trial. JAMA Psychiatry, 80(1), 13-21. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.3679>
- 备注：试验用的是标准化的 8 周正念减压课程（MBSR），随便一个冥想 App 不能和它划等号。它适用于已经达到焦虑障碍诊断标准的人，该看医生还是要看。如果你因为顾虑药物副作用一直拖着不治疗，这是一个证据强度相当的选项。
  Note: The trial used a standardized 8-week mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR) course — a random meditation app is not equivalent. It applies to people who meet diagnostic criteria for an anxiety disorder; you should still see a doctor. If concerns about drug side effects have kept you from getting treated, this is an option with comparable evidence strength.

### 8. 当场想缓过来，用 5 分钟「循环叹息」：吸气两段，呼气拉长 / To calm down on the spot, try 5 minutes of "cyclic sighing": two-part inhale, long exhale
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- 成本：不花钱。每天 5 分钟。
  Cost: Free. Five minutes a day.
- 说人话：每天做 5 分钟呼吸练习、坚持一个月，情绪改善和呼吸放慢都比同样时长的正念冥想好。几种呼吸法里，把呼气拉长的「循环叹息」最好。做法是用鼻子吸一口，再补吸一小口把肺撑满，然后用嘴慢慢吐尽。这只是一项试验，好坏靠本人自己报，但不花钱。
  In plain terms: Doing 5 minutes of breathing exercises daily for a month improved mood and slowed breathing more than the same amount of mindfulness meditation. Among breathing techniques, "cyclic sighing" — with the emphasis on lengthening the exhale — worked best. The technique: inhale through the nose, take a short second sip to fill the lungs, then exhale slowly and fully through the mouth. This is just one trial, with outcomes self-reported — but it's free.
- 收益：这是一项在家做、随机分组的对照试验，比较三种每天 5 分钟的呼吸练习和同样时长的正念冥想，持续 1 个月。三种呼吸法是：循环叹息（强调延长呼气）、箱式呼吸（吸气、屏息、呼气等长）、带屏息的循环过度换气（吸气长、呼气短）。把各人各次的数据放在一起算（混合效应模型），呼吸练习都好过正念冥想，尤其是以呼气为主的循环叹息。它在情绪改善（P<0.05，即这点差别不像是碰巧撞出来的）和呼吸频率下降（P<0.05）上都更好。
  Benefit: This was a randomized controlled trial done at home, comparing three 5-minute daily breathing practices against the same amount of mindfulness meditation over 1 month. The three techniques: cyclic sighing (emphasizing extended exhale), box breathing (equal inhale, hold, exhale), and cyclic hyperventilation with breath retention (long inhale, short exhale). Pooling each person's repeated measurements (mixed-effects model), all breathing practices beat mindfulness meditation, especially exhale-focused cyclic sighing — better on mood improvement (P<0.05, i.e., the difference is unlikely to be chance) and on reduced respiratory rate (P<0.05).
- 证据等级：B / Evidence grade: B
- 来源：Balban MY, Neri E, Kogon MM, et al. (2023). Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal. Cell Reports Medicine, 4(1), 100895. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895>
- 备注：做法是用鼻子吸一口，短暂再补吸一小口把肺撑满，然后用嘴把气缓慢长长地吐尽，重复 5 分钟。只有这一项试验，在家做，好不好由本人填问卷报，人数也不多。效果只是比冥想略好，解决不了问题。但它不花钱，只要 5 分钟，随时随地能做，性价比高。
  Note: The technique: inhale through the nose, take a short second sip to fill the lungs, then exhale slowly and fully through the mouth, repeating for 5 minutes. Only one trial, done at home, with self-reported questionnaires and a modest sample. The effect is only slightly better than meditation and won't solve problems. But it's free, takes 5 minutes, and can be done anywhere — great value for money.

### 9. 把「定期和人见面」当成健康支出，别只在心情差时才找人 / Treat "meeting people regularly" as a health expense — don't only reach out when you're down
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- 成本：要花一点钱，还要搭上见面的时间。难在心情好的时候也得主动约人。
  Cost: Costs a little money, plus the time to meet. The hard part is reaching out even when you feel fine.
- 说人话：平时很少和人来往的人，同期死亡的概率高约三成。感到孤独的人高约四分之一，独居的人高约三成，65 岁以下的人更明显。这些都是跟踪观察出来的，也可能是身体本来就差的人更容易独居。
  In plain terms: People who rarely interact with others have about a 30% higher chance of dying in the same period. For those who feel lonely it's about a quarter higher; for those living alone about 30% higher, and the pattern is stronger in people under 65. These all come from follow-up observations — it could also be that people in poorer health are more likely to end up living alone.
- 收益：这是把 70 项先记录情况再往后跟踪的研究合在一起算的结果（荟萃分析），共 340 万名参与者，平均跟踪 7 年。社会隔离、孤独感、独居都与死亡率升高相关。把年龄、身体状况这些干扰因素扣掉之后，加权平均比值比分别是：社会隔离 1.29、孤独 1.26、独居 1.32。换算过来，同期死亡的概率分别高约三成、约四分之一、约三成。三者的效果大小差不多，而且在 65 岁以下人群中比在老年人群中更大。
  Benefit: This pools 70 studies that recorded people's situations first and then followed them over time (a meta-analysis): 3.4 million participants, average follow-up 7 years. Social isolation, loneliness, and living alone were all linked to higher mortality. After adjusting for age, health status, and other confounders, the weighted average odds ratios were: social isolation 1.29, loneliness 1.26, living alone 1.32. Converted, the chance of dying in the same period was about 30%, about a quarter, and about 30% higher respectively. The three effect sizes were similar, and larger in people under 65 than in older adults.
- 证据等级：A / Evidence grade: A
- 来源：Holt-Lunstad J, Smith TB, Baker M, Harris T, Stephenson D (2015). Loneliness and social isolation as risk factors for mortality: a meta-analytic review. Perspectives on Psychological Science, 10(2), 227-237. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691614568352>
- 备注：争议。这些都是跟着人群观察出来的关联，不是分组对照试验，谁因谁果分不开。身体本来就差的人，更容易独居、更少社交。作者把社会关系看作和吸烟、肥胖同一量级的健康因素，但没有随机试验证明「增加社交能降低死亡率」。这条与第 3 节「减少让你消耗的人际关系」不冲突：那条砍掉消耗你的关系，这里要保住让你恢复精力的那几段。
  Note: Contested. These are associations observed by following people over time, not randomized trials — cause and effect can't be separated. People in poorer health are more likely to live alone and socialize less. The authors treat social relationships as a health factor on par with smoking and obesity, but no randomized trial proves that "more socializing lowers mortality." This item doesn't conflict with section 3's "cut relationships that drain you": that one cuts draining ties, this one keeps the few that restore you.

### 10. 住得离绿地近就多用它，散步、遛弯、坐着都算 / If you live near green space, use it — walking, strolling, or just sitting all count
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- 成本：不花钱。顺路就做了。
  Cost: Free. It happens on your way.
- 说人话：住处周边 500 米内的绿化越好，同期死亡的概率越低，按植被指数每提高 0.1 算，低约 4%。量的是住处周边的绿化，不是你去公园的次数。绿化好的地段，住的人往往更有钱、空气也更好。所以别当成去公园就能延寿，选住处时把周边绿地算进去就行。
  In plain terms: The greener the area within 500 meters of your home, the lower the chance of dying in the same period — about 4% lower for each 0.1 increase in the vegetation index. What was measured is greenery around the home, not how often you visit parks. Greener neighborhoods also tend to house wealthier people with cleaner air. So don't read this as "visiting parks extends life" — just factor nearby green space into your choice of where to live.
- 收益：这是把 9 项跟踪人群多年的研究合在一起算的结果（队列研究的荟萃分析），涉及 7 个国家、832 万人。看的是住处 500 米以内的周边绿化程度，用归一化植被指数（NDVI，把地面绿化折算成的一个数）衡量。绿化程度每增加 0.1 个指数，总死亡率的合并风险比为 0.96（95% CI 0.94–0.97），同期死亡的概率低约 4%，真实值大致落在低 3% 到低 6% 这一段。9 项研究中有 7 项发现绿化越好死亡率越低，差别也不像碰巧。另外 2 项没看出关联。
  Benefit: This pools 9 studies that followed populations for years (a meta-analysis of cohort studies): 7 countries, 8.32 million people. It looked at greenery within 500 meters of home, measured by the normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI — a number that converts ground greenery into a score). For each 0.1 increase in greenery, the pooled hazard ratio for total mortality was 0.96 (95% CI 0.94–0.97): the chance of dying in the same period was about 4% lower, with the true value roughly between 3% and 6% lower. 7 of the 9 studies found lower mortality with more greenery, and the difference doesn't look like chance. The other 2 found no link.
- 证据等级：A / Evidence grade: A
- 来源：Rojas-Rueda D, Nieuwenhuijsen MJ, Gascon M, Perez-Leon D, Mudu P (2019). Green spaces and mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies. The Lancet Planetary Health, 3(11), e469-e477. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2542-5196(19)30215-3>
- 备注：争议。各项研究的结果差得很远（I²=95%，这个数越大越是彼此对不上）。而且它测的是居住地周边的绿化程度，和你去公园的次数无关。住在绿化好的地方的人往往也更富、空气更好、更爱运动，这几样和绿化分不开谁在起作用。别读成「去公园就能延寿」，可用的部分是选住处时把绿地算进去，成本为零。NDVI 这个数，裸地和水泥接近 0，茂密植被接近 1。所以「0.1」大致是从光秃街区挪到有成排行道树的街区那种差别。
  Note: Contested. The studies' results vary widely (I²=95% — the bigger this number, the less the studies agree). And it measured greenery around the home, unrelated to how often you visit parks. People in greener areas also tend to be wealthier, breathe cleaner air, and exercise more — these can't be separated from greenery as to what's doing the work. Don't read it as "parks extend life"; the usable part is factoring green space into where you live, at zero cost. On the NDVI scale, bare ground and concrete are near 0 and dense vegetation near 1. So "0.1" is roughly the difference between a bare block and one lined with street trees.
